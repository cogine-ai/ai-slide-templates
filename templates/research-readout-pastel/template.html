METHODOLOGY
TIMELINE
3 weeks
We followed the planning
journey from the first idea
to the day of travel, across
three consecutive weekends.
PANEL SIZE
18 people
Weekend travellers across
three planning styles, with
recent group-trip experience:

• Independent explorers
• Group organisers
• Frequent city visitors
APPROACH
12 sessions
Context interviews and
moderated prototype tasks
showed where people pause.

Six follow-up diaries kept
the findings grounded in
real plans and decisions.

KEY FINDINGS
People want a starting
point, not a strict script.
A little structure reduces the effort of getting started.
Too much detail makes the weekend feel fixed before
it has even begun.

People trust a shared plan when they can see what
is decided, what is flexible and who added it.
Finding 1
Start with one useful day, rather than a
blank canvas or a packed itinerary.
Finding 2
Make preferences visible before asking
the group to choose.
Finding 3
Keep the details close to the decision
that needs them.
NOVEMBER 2026
FIELDWORK
FINDING 1
A useful first draft
makes it easier
to get started.
People could react to a suggested day much faster
than they could build one from a blank screen.
A draft gives the conversation something concrete.
The revised screen offers three possible stops, with
a clear way to move, remove or add a place.
NOVEMBER 2026
FIELDWORK
FINDING 2
A shared plan works
when preferences
are visible.
Groups often discussed logistics before they had
shared what each person hoped to do. Showing
preferences helped the group make a calmer choice.
The concept makes the people behind each saved
place visible, while keeping the day easy to scan.
NOVEMBER 2026
FIELDWORK
FINDING 3
Flexibility needs
to feel deliberate,
not unfinished.
People welcomed an open afternoon when they
could see that the key details were already covered.
Uncertainty about bookings created the tension.
The prototype separates fixed reservations from
flexible ideas without hiding either from the group.
NOVEMBER 2026
FIELDWORK
FINDING 4
Keep the next detail
close to the moment
that needs it.
The effort was rarely finding another place to go.
It was finding the right address, booking or message
when the group was ready to move.
A connected day view brings those details into one
place, so the plan remains useful during the trip.

RECOMMENDATIONS
Finding 1
People responded faster when there was a useful
starting point, with enough context to understand
what could still change.
Recommendation
Offer one useful
first day, then
make it easy to
change together.
NOVEMBER 2026
FIELDWORK
RECOMMENDATIONS
Finding 2
The group made clearer decisions when each
person’s priorities were visible beside the options,
rather than hidden in a separate conversation.
Recommendation
Show who saved
each place, and
what they hope
to do there.

SUMMARY
Here’s what we recommend:
Start with a useful day, then make
it easy for the whole group to
keep the weekend open.
Bring fixed reservations, flexible ideas and
people’s preferences into one shared view.
Keep the next useful detail close.
Next: test the revised day view with six groups,
then review what they change during the trip.
